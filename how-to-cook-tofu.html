How to Cook Tofu: A Beginner's Guide

Tofu has a reputation for being bland or mushy, but that usually comes from skipping a step or choosing the wrong type. Follow this guide and you will get tofu that is crisp, creamy, or tender on purpose.

Step 1: Choose the Right Tofu

Buy the type that matches your method. Extra-firm and firm tofu are the best starting point for frying, baking, grilling, and air frying. Soft and silken tofu belong in soups, sauces, and desserts. If a recipe does not say, firm is the safest choice.

Step 2: Press It (When It Is Firm)

Pressing removes water so the tofu can brown and soak up flavor.

  1. Drain the block and wrap it in a clean towel or several paper towels.
  2. Place it on a plate and set something heavy on top, like a cast iron pan or a few cans.
  3. Wait 15 to 30 minutes, then pat dry.

Skip pressing for silken and soft tofu, and for tofu you plan to crumble into a scramble where a little moisture is fine.

Step 3: Cut It for the Texture You Want

  • Cubes: Good for stir-fries, baking, and air frying.
  • Slabs or planks: Good for sandwiches, grilling, and pan-searing.
  • Crumbles: Tear by hand for scrambles, tacos, and fillings. Rough edges crisp up nicely.
  • Triangles: Cut a slab diagonally for soups and braises.

Step 4: Pick a Cooking Method

Pan-frying

Heat 1 to 2 tablespoons of oil in a nonstick or cast iron pan over medium-high heat. Add dry tofu in a single layer and leave it alone for 3 to 4 minutes until it releases easily. Flip and repeat. Total time is about 10 minutes.

Baking

Toss cubes with oil and seasoning and spread on a lined tray. Bake at 400°F for 25 to 35 minutes, flipping halfway. Baked tofu is chewy and works well for meal prep.

Air frying

Cook coated cubes at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking halfway. It is the quickest route to a crunchy outside. See our full air fryer guide.

Simmering and braising

Add soft or firm tofu to broth, curry, or sauce and simmer 5 to 15 minutes. The tofu absorbs the liquid and turns tender and flavorful.

Scrambling

Crumble firm or medium tofu into a hot oiled pan with onion, turmeric, and salt. Cook 6 to 8 minutes until the moisture cooks off.

Steaming or serving silken

Steam silken tofu for 5 minutes and top with soy sauce, scallions, and chili oil. Or serve it cold with a drizzle of sesame oil and a pinch of salt.

Step 5: Season Boldly

Tofu will not taste like much on its own, so give it help. Good building blocks include:

  • Salty: soy sauce, tamari, miso, or plain salt
  • Acid: rice vinegar, lime, or lemon
  • Heat: chili flakes, chili paste, or hot sauce
  • Aromatics: garlic, ginger, scallion, or onion powder
  • Depth: smoked paprika, toasted sesame oil, or nutritional yeast

A short 10-minute marinade helps, and tossing the cooked tofu in sauce at the end keeps it crisp.

Common Mistakes

  • Skipping the press on firm tofu, which leads to steaming instead of browning.
  • Crowding the pan or tray, so the pieces stew in their own moisture.
  • Moving the tofu too early. Wait until it releases from the pan on its own.
  • Using firm tofu in a dish that needs silken, or the other way around.
  • Under-seasoning. Taste and adjust.

Storing Tofu

Keep unopened tofu in its package until the date printed on it. Once opened, store leftover tofu in a container covered with fresh water, change the water daily, and use within three to four days. Cooked tofu keeps about four days in the fridge. You can also freeze a block; after thawing, it becomes chewier and soaks up marinades more readily.
